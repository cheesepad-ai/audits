### Medium Findings

#### 🟡 [M-1] Entire supply minted to deployer creates concentration / rug-pull risk

**Description:**
The constructor mints the full scaled supply to `msg.sender` with no vesting, timelock, or distribution mechanism.

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

The deployer initially controls 100% of tokens. If these are added to a liquidity pool, the deployer can withdraw liquidity or dump the full holding at any time.

**Impact:**
Holders are exposed to price collapse from a single-address sell-off or liquidity removal. This is a centralization/economic risk rather than a code vulnerability.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Verify off-chain how the deployer distributes and locks liquidity. Consider a vesting contract or LP timelock, and publish the initial holder distribution for transparency.

---

#### 🟡 [M-2] Unbounded `totalSupply_` multiplication can silently revert or produce dust configs

**Description:**
`totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_))` is performed in checked arithmetic. A large `totalSupply_` combined with `decimals_` up to 18 can overflow `uint256` and revert the deployment. More subtly, there is no validation that the resulting supply is non-zero, allowing a zero-supply token to be deployed.

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
```

**Impact:**
Deployment reverts (fee already refunded via revert, so low direct fund risk), or a degenerate token with zero supply can be created, wasting the deployment fee.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Add `require(totalSupply_ > 0, "Zero supply")` and document the maximum safe `totalSupply_`/`decimals_` combination to avoid confusing revert-on-deploy behavior.

#### 🟢 [L-1] Fee forwarded via low-level call to arbitrary receiver in constructor

**Description:**
The constructor forwards `msg.value` to a caller-supplied `feeReceiver_` using a low-level `call`.

```solidity
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
require(ok, "Fee transfer failed");
```

Because `_mint` runs after the external call, and no state that the receiver could re-enter into exists (no mint function, single-shot constructor), reentrancy is not exploitable. However, the fee receiver is fully attacker-controllable at deploy time and could be a contract that consumes unbounded gas.

**Impact:**
Minimal — deployer controls both the value and the receiver. Griefing is self-inflicted. Noted for completeness.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Since `feeReceiver_` is typically the platform, consider making it an immutable/hardcoded address rather than a constructor parameter to prevent fee bypass by passing `feeReceiver_ == msg.sender`.

#### 🟢 [L-2] Fee can be trivially bypassed

**Description:**
`feeReceiver_` and `feeAmount_` are both deployer-supplied. A deployer can pass `feeAmount_ = 0` (making `msg.value == 0` valid) or set `feeReceiver_` to their own address, receiving the fee back.

**Impact:**
If this contract is intended as a paid launchpad template, the fee mechanism provides no guarantee of payment to the platform.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor, `require(msg.value == feeAmount_, ...)`.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Enforce the fee amount and receiver from a trusted factory/registry rather than accepting them as free constructor arguments.

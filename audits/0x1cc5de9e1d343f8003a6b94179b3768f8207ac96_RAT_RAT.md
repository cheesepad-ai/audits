### Medium Findings

#### 🟡 [M-1] Unbounded `totalSupply_` multiplication can revert or enable arbitrarily large supply

**Description:**
The constructor scales the raw supply by decimals without any upper bound:

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

There is no cap on `totalSupply_`. A large `totalSupply_` combined with `decimals_ = 18` can overflow and revert the deployment (Solidity ^0.8 reverts on overflow, so no silent wrap), and there is no minimum or maximum sanity check. While not exploitable post-deploy (supply is fixed), a deployer could mint an arbitrarily huge supply, which affects downstream integrations and price/liquidity assumptions.

**Impact:**
Deployer can create tokens with practically unlimited supply; extreme inputs revert deployment. Integrators relying on a bounded supply have no on-chain guarantee.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor, `scaledSupply` computation.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Add an explicit maximum supply bound (e.g., `require(totalSupply_ <= MAX_SUPPLY, "Supply too large")`) or document that supply is fully deployer-controlled and unbounded. Validate `totalSupply_ > 0` if a zero supply is undesirable.

### Low Findings

#### 🟢 [L-1] Excess `msg.value` requires exact match and no refund path

**Description:**
The constructor enforces `require(msg.value == feeAmount_, "Invalid fee value")` and forwards the entire `msg.value` to the fee receiver. Because `feeAmount_` is caller-supplied and equals `msg.value`, the fee is entirely self-declared by the deployer.

```solidity
require(msg.value == feeAmount_, "Invalid fee value");
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
```

Since the deployer sets both `feeAmount_` and `msg.value`, the "fee" provides no protocol-enforced value — a deployer can pass `feeAmount_ = 0` and pay nothing.

**Impact:**
The fee mechanism is trivially bypassable and offers no economic guarantee to any platform expecting a fee. Purely a design weakness, no direct fund loss to users.

**Location:**
Constructor fee logic.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** If a real fee is intended, hardcode or govern `feeAmount_` via an authoritative source (constant or trusted registry) rather than accepting it as a caller parameter.

#### 🟢 [L-2] Fee transfer to arbitrary `feeReceiver_` with low-level call in constructor

**Description:**
The constructor forwards ETH via a low-level `call` to a caller-supplied `feeReceiver_`. Although the mint occurs after the call (constructor state is otherwise isolated), forwarding funds to an arbitrary address using `call` with no gas limit is a minor concern and could interact with a malicious contract during deployment.

```solidity
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
require(ok, "Fee transfer failed");
```

**Impact:**
Limited — reentrancy is not meaningful here since token state (`_mint`) follows and no external-callable state is exposed mid-construction. Primarily a code-hygiene note.

**Location:**
Constructor, fee transfer.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Perform the fee transfer after all critical state initialization (already largely the case), and consider validating `feeReceiver_` is an expected address if a fixed recipient is intended.

#### 🟢 [L-3] No event emitted for deployment fee payment

**Description:**
The constructor transfers a fee but emits no event recording the fee amount or receiver. Off-chain systems and auditors cannot easily track fee payments.

**Impact:**
Reduced transparency and off-chain traceability of fee flows. No security impact.

**Location:**

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Emit an event such as `FeePaid(feeReceiver_, feeAmount_)` after a successful fee transfer.

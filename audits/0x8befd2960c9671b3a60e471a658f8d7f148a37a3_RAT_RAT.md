### Medium Findings

#### 🟡 [M-1] Entire Supply Minted to Deployer (Centralization / Rug Risk)

**Description:**
The full token supply is minted to `msg.sender` in the constructor, with no vesting, timelock, or distribution logic.

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

The deployer therefore controls 100% of the 1,000,000 RAT supply immediately after deployment.

**Impact:**
The deployer can dump the entire supply on any liquidity pool, causing catastrophic price impact for holders (classic rug-pull vector). Token distribution fairness depends entirely on off-chain deployer behavior.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Verify on-chain how the deployer's balance is distributed (liquidity, locks, treasury). For fair-launch guarantees, use a vesting/timelock contract or lock liquidity, and publicly document allocation. Buyers should treat concentrated supply as a material risk.

#### 🟢 [L-2] Payable Constructor with Strict `msg.value == feeAmount_` Coupling

**Description:**
The constructor requires exact `msg.value == feeAmount_` and forwards it entirely to `feeReceiver_`. `feeAmount_` is caller-supplied, so the fee is self-declared rather than enforced by a protocol.

```solidity
require(msg.value == feeAmount_, "Invalid fee value");
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
require(ok, "Fee transfer failed");
```

**Impact:**
A deployer could set `feeAmount_ = 0`, bypassing any intended launchpad fee. The "fee" provides no security guarantee and is trivially avoidable if the deployer controls the parameters.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** If the fee is meant to be enforced by a platform, hardcode or validate `feeReceiver_` and `feeAmount_` against trusted values rather than accepting arbitrary constructor inputs.

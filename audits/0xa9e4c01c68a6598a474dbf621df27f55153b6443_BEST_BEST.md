### Medium Findings

#### 🟡 [M-1] Unbounded Supply Multiplication Can Silently Revert or Cause Deployment Failure

**Description:**
The constructor scales the supply without any upper bound check:

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

While Solidity 0.8+ reverts on overflow (preventing a silent wrap), a caller passing a large `totalSupply_` combined with high `decimals_` (up to 18) can cause the multiplication to overflow `uint256` and revert, wasting the paid deployment fee if the fee transfer succeeds before the revert — though here the mint occurs after the fee transfer, so the entire transaction reverts and the fee is returned. The lack of a documented maximum supply cap and validation of `totalSupply_` (e.g., disallowing zero) may lead to accidental misconfiguration (zero-supply token or unexpectedly huge supply).

**Impact:**
Misconfigured deployments may revert unexpectedly or produce a zero-supply / unreasonably large token. No fund loss occurs since the whole tx reverts atomically, but poor UX and potential wasted gas.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Add sanity validation such as `require(totalSupply_ > 0, "Zero supply")` and consider an explicit upper bound on `scaledSupply`. Document the intended maximum supply range for callers.

### Low Findings

#### 🟢 [L-1] Deployment Fee Sent Before Mint — Ordering / CEI Consideration

**Description:**
The constructor performs an external native-currency call to `feeReceiver_` before completing minting:

```solidity
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
require(ok, "Fee transfer failed");
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

Because this occurs inside the constructor, contract code is not yet deployed and reentrancy into token functions is not exploitable. However, sending funds before all state is finalized deviates from strict checks-effects-interactions ordering. If `feeReceiver_` is a contract that reverts, the whole deployment fails.

**Impact:**
Low. No exploitable reentrancy in a constructor context, but a malicious or misconfigured `feeReceiver_` can grief deployment.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Perform the fee transfer as the last action in the constructor (after `_mint`) to follow checks-effects-interactions, or clearly document that `feeReceiver_` must be a trusted, non-reverting address.

#### 🟢 [L-2] Fee Handling Requires Exact `msg.value` Match

**Description:**
The constructor enforces an exact fee equality:

```solidity
require(msg.value == feeAmount_, "Invalid fee value");
```

Since `feeAmount_` is caller-supplied and must equal `msg.value`, the check is effectively self-referential and provides no protocol-level fee enforcement — the deployer can pass `feeAmount_ = 0` with `msg.value = 0` and pay nothing. The "fee" is only meaningful if enforced by an external factory.

**Impact:**
Low. The fee mechanism offers no on-chain guarantee of a minimum fee; it relies entirely on an off-chain/factory caller supplying the correct `feeAmount_`.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** If a minimum fee is intended, hardcode or validate `feeAmount_` against a required minimum, or enforce fees at the deploying factory level and document this design.

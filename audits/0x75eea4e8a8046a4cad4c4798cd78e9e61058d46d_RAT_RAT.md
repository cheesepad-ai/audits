### Medium Findings

#### 🟡 [M-1] Entire supply minted to deployer with no distribution safeguards

**Description:**
The constructor mints 100% of the token supply to `msg.sender` (the deployer):

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

There is no vesting, timelock, liquidity lock, or multi-recipient distribution. A single externally owned address controls the full 1e14 token supply and can dump or withdraw liquidity at will.

**Impact:**
High supply concentration is a classic rug-pull / dump vector. Holders and liquidity providers are exposed to the deployer's discretion; there are no on-chain guarantees against a sudden sell-off.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Verify off-chain that deployer tokens are locked (liquidity locker / vesting contract) and that ownership of the deployer wallet is trustworthy. For future deployments, consider distributing supply across a vesting/timelock contract rather than a single EOA.

### Low Findings

#### 🟢 [L-1] Unbounded `totalSupply_` allows silent overflow-adjacent scaling errors

**Description:**
`totalSupply_` is multiplied by `10 ** decimals_` without an upper bound check:

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
```

While Solidity ≥0.8 reverts on overflow (so no unsafe wraparound occurs), an extreme `totalSupply_` combined with `decimals_ = 18` can revert the deployment with an unclear panic, or produce an economically nonsensical supply if within bounds.

**Impact:**
Deployment reverts with a generic arithmetic panic rather than a descriptive error; no funds lost, but poor UX and potential for accidental misconfiguration.

**Location:**
Constructor, `scaledSupply` computation.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Add a sanity bound on `totalSupply_` (e.g., `require(totalSupply_ <= MAX_RAW_SUPPLY, "Supply too large")`) with a clear revert message.

#### 🟢 [L-2] Deployment fee sent via low-level call before completing state setup

**Description:**
The constructor forwards `msg.value` to `feeReceiver_` using a low-level `call` before minting:

```solidity
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
require(ok, "Fee transfer failed");
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

Because this occurs during construction and no critical state depends on the callee's behavior, reentrancy risk is negligible. However, a malicious or contract `feeReceiver_` could consume gas or grief the deployer, and the ordering deviates from checks-effects-interactions.

**Impact:**
Minimal — limited to deployment-time griefing by the fee receiver. No post-deploy attack surface.

**Location:**
Constructor fee transfer.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Perform the external fee transfer last (after `_mint`) to follow checks-effects-interactions, or accept as-is given constructor context and trusted launchpad receiver.

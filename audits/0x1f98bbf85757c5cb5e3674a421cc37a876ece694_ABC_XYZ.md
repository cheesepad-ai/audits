### Medium Findings

#### 🟡 [M-1] Entire Supply Minted to Deployer (Distribution Centralization)

**Description:**
The constructor mints the full supply to `msg.sender` with no vesting, lock, or distribution logic.

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

At deployment 100% of `totalSupply` sits in a single externally owned account.

**Impact:**
The deployer can dump the entire supply, control liquidity, or rug-pull holders. Buyers on secondary markets have no on-chain protection against unilateral supply movement. This is a common characteristic of low-trust launches.

**Location:**
`CheesePadStandardToken` constructor.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Implement transparent distribution: lock deployer/team allocations via a timelock or vesting contract, and lock liquidity. Publicly document the token distribution so holders can verify supply concentration before purchasing.

**Description:**
`totalSupply_` is multiplied by `10 ** decimals_` with no upper bound. A very large `totalSupply_` combined with 18 decimals could overflow the checked multiplication and revert deployment, or produce a supply far larger than intended if the deployer miscalculates units.

```solidity
uint256 scaledSupply = totalSupply_ * (10 ** uint256(decimals_));
```

**Impact:**
Deployment-time revert (self-inflicted, no fund loss) or an unintended supply if the deployer passes an already-scaled value.

**Location:**
Constructor, `scaledSupply` computation.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Clearly document whether `totalSupply_` is expected in whole tokens (pre-scaling). Consider validating a sane maximum to prevent accidental over-issuance.

#### 🟢 [L-2] Fee Transfer via Low-Level Call Without Reentrancy Guard

**Description:**
The constructor makes a low-level `call` forwarding `msg.value` to an arbitrary `feeReceiver_` before minting completes.

```solidity
(bool ok, ) = feeReceiver_.call{value: msg.value}("");
require(ok, "Fee transfer failed");
_mint(msg.sender, scaledSupply);
```

Because this happens in the constructor, no external contract can reenter meaningfully (contract not yet deployed / no state to exploit), so risk is minimal. However, a malicious or misconfigured `feeReceiver_` that reverts will brick deployment.

**Impact:**
A reverting fee receiver blocks deployment. No fund-loss vector post-deploy since the call is constructor-only.

**Location:**
Constructor fee forwarding.

**💡 Recommendation:**
> **Action Required:** Acceptable as constructor-only, but confirm `feeReceiver_` is a trusted, payable-accepting address. Consider a pull-based fee pattern if third parties will deploy through this contract.
